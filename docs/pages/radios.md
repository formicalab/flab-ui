# Wi-Fi radios (route `#radios`)

The radios as hardware, to read and to change: a card per radio, refreshed every 5 s. The wireless networks (SSIDs) of the radios are on the [Wi-Fi](wifi.md) page; the connected clients on the [Connected devices](connected.md) tab of Devices and Interfaces. The page is `radios/index.js`, the cards `radios/card.js`, the display parts `radios/parts.js`; the editing (the schema, the model, the controls, the channel picker) is in `wifi/` (`schema.js`, `model.js`, `editors.js`, `channels.js`, `picker.js`) and is shared with the Wi-Fi page, as is the draft and the review banner (`wifi/editor.js`): an edit made here is still pending when the Wi-Fi page is opened, and one Save applies both.

**Reading** (`flab.radio`): `status` gives the radios, `config` the wireless configuration. A card has the header (state dot, name, band, phy, state, supported standards, a link to each mesh interface on the radio, the hardware name), the key figures (running channel and frequency, width and center, TX power in dBm and mW, noise floor, antenna chains, clients), the findings when something is off (radio down, setup failing, a running channel different from the configured one, high noise, a busy channel, a DFS availability check in progress, an open network, a country that differs from the regulatory domain), the configuration next to what is actually running (channel, mode, TX power with the regulatory limit of that channel, country, cell density, beacon interval, options, the device path), the channel utilization (busy, RX and TX airtime as a percentage and a chart, derived in the browser from the driver's cumulative survey counters) and the channels allowed by the regulatory domain: the picker (below), or a plain strip with the running block highlighted and the DFS (radar) channels marked when the configuration cannot be edited. Tiles above the cards: radios up, wireless interfaces, clients, the busiest channel. `status` copies only whitelisted options: keys, passwords, FT key material and MAC lists never leave the router through it.

**Radio settings**: a foldable block per card with the options of a `wifi-device` section of the [OpenWrt wireless documentation](https://openwrt.org/docs/guide-user/network/wifi/basic), each with a hint taken from it, in three groups:
- **Radio**: disable the radio, mode and channel width (`htmode`), the channels automatic selection may use (`channels`, a list of channels and ranges, shown while the channel is automatic), country, transmit power (`txpower`).
- **Rates and coexistence**: cell density, legacy 802.11b rates, minimum client standard (`require_mode`), the coexistence scan (`noscan`) and the HT coexistence flag (`ht_coex`) on 2.4 GHz, beacon interval, the basic and supported rates (`basic_rate`, `supported_rates`, lists in kb/s that override the cell density).
- **Advanced**: the country advertisement (`country_ie`, 802.11d), distance, RTS and fragmentation thresholds, antenna gain, the hostapd log level, automatic antenna selection and the receiving and transmitting antenna (`diversity`, `rxantenna`, `txantenna`, used by some drivers only), the narrow channel width (`chanbw`, 5, 10 or 20 MHz; ath9k and ath5k only, so it is shown only where the file already sets it), the high throughput capabilities (`ht_capab`, detected by themselves) and extra hostapd options (`hostapd_options`, passed as they are).
The block also says how the radio is identified in the file (`type`, `phy`, `radio`, `macaddr`, `path`, `band`: detected by themselves, not edited here). The mode list is what the hardware reports: the htmodes `iwinfo` lists for a running interface, else the widths the phy's capabilities advertise for the radio's band (HT, VHT, HE, EHT; the explicit `HT40+` and `HT40-` on 2.4 GHz and `NOHT` are added); a radio whose phy is unknown gets a text box checked for its form. The country list comes from the driver (`iwinfo <if> countrylist`), else a text box. Options that apply to one band are shown on it (legacy rates, the coexistence scan and the HT coexistence flag on 2.4 GHz) or when they are already set. The lists are validated one entry at a time (a channel or a range such as `36-48`, a rate in kb/s, a capability such as `SHORT-GI-40`).
**The channel picker** (`wifi/channels.js` model, `wifi/picker.js` control): each radio card shows its allowed channels as tiles. The
`channel` option of the radio is a channel number or `auto`; a channel is only selectable when the whole block of the width of the mode
being edited fits around it in the regulatory domain (others are hatched), so choosing a mode redraws which blocks fit, and a mode that
no longer fits the chosen channel is an error on both controls.
- **Preview**: hovering or focusing a tile shows the block the radio would use at that width, in blue,
  or in red with the reason when it does not fit. The block is made of whole 20 MHz channels: on 5 and 6 GHz the
  fixed blocks (36-48 at 80 MHz, 100-128 at 160 MHz); on 2.4 GHz a 40 MHz block is the channel and the one four
  numbers away, on the side netifd picks (channels 1-6 above, 7-13 below, or an explicit `HT40+`/`HT40-`), so
  channel 4 with channel 8 covers 2417-2457 MHz. The channels between the two are shown as covered, the ones that
  stick out as partly overlapped (dashed). The sentence under the tiles says the same in words.
- **Choosing**: a click selects (green, with its block tinted), a second click undoes it; the Auto chip hands the
  choice to the radio. The yellow review banner lists the change from and to, with warnings that do not block Save:
  a block that reaches radar (DFS) channels (the radio listens for radar for 1 to 10 minutes before clients can
  connect), `auto`, and a radio that carries a mesh interface (the other mesh nodes must be moved to the same
  channel by hand). A channel the regulatory domain disables, or whose block does not fit, is an error that
  blocks Save.

**Validation and saving** are those of the wireless editor described on the [Wi-Fi](wifi.md) page (secrets are never read, only what the draft changes is checked, errors block Save, warnings do not, the change is staged, applied with the rollback countdown, read back through `flab.radio config` and the radios must come up). The radio errors: a mode the hardware does not report, a country the driver does not know, a channel the new mode cannot carry, numbers out of range, a malformed channel, rate or capability. The radio warnings: switching a radio off (how many networks and clients it takes down), changing the country, a power above the regulatory limit of the channel, a 40 MHz channel on 2.4 GHz, legacy rates, requiring 802.11ac, a block that reaches radar channels. Polling is suspended while edits are pending, a control has the focus or the pointer is over the channel picker. A login that may not write gets the settings read only with a note; a router whose `flab.radio` has no `config` method gets the radios read only with a note and the plain channel strip.
